# lokul.club — Investor Outreach Email Templates
### First Contact, Warm Intros, Follow-Ups

> The email doesn't close the deal. It earns the meeting.
> Every word should serve one purpose: get a reply that says "let's talk."

---

## The Golden Rules Before Writing Anything

1. **Under 150 words for cold email.** If you can't explain it in 150 words, you don't understand it well enough yet.
2. **No deck in the first email.** It signals you don't trust your words and gives them a reason to say no without meeting you.
3. **One ask only.** "30 minutes this week or next" — not "a meeting, or a call, or feedback on my deck."
4. **Show you know them.** One specific line about their portfolio or thesis. Not flattery — relevance.
5. **Lead with the most surprising fact you have.** Not the product description. The insight that stops scrolling.

## Template 1: Cold Email — No Prior Connection

**When to use:** Reaching out to an investor you've never met, no mutual connection.

---

**Subject:** lokul — India's 90M urban households, no neighborhood OS yet

**Body:**

Hi [Name],

India has 90 million urban households who transact locally every day — tiffins, tutors, plumbers, kirana — almost entirely through WhatsApp chaos and word-of-mouth. No platform has cracked the trust layer that makes this transactable.

lokul.club is a geo-verified community platform where neighbors discover, trust, and transact with each other and local merchants. We're building the neighborhood OS that MyGate proved residents want but never monetized.

We're raising a $750K pre-seed. [One traction line: e.g., "Early pilot across X societies, ₹Y GMV, Z% month-on-month growth."]

I noticed your investment in [specific portfolio company] — I think the community-commerce thesis maps directly to what we're building.

Would you have 30 minutes this week or next?

[Your name]
lokul.club | [phone]

---

**Why this works:**
- Line 1 opens with market insight, not product description
- Line 2 says what it is in one sentence
- Line 3 anchors on a known name (MyGate) — instant frame of reference
- The traction line is the only proof point — keep it to one
- Specific portfolio callout shows homework, not spam

## Template 2: Warm Intro Email — What You Send to the Connector

**When to use:** You have a mutual connection. You send this to THEM and ask them to forward it.

> The warm intro email is not for the investor. It's for the person making the intro.
> Make it so easy to forward that they only need to add "thought you'd find this interesting."

---

**Subject:** Intro request — lokul.club (hyperlocal community platform, pre-seed)

**Body:**

Hi [Connector's name],

I hope you're well. I'm raising a pre-seed round for lokul.club — a geo-verified neighborhood platform for India that turns local communities into commerce networks.

I'd love a warm intro to [Investor name] at [Fund]. Their investment in [portfolio company] suggests they understand community-led commerce, and I think there's a strong thesis match.

I've written the forwarding email below — feel free to edit it. Happy to give you more context on the company first if helpful.

Thanks so much,
[Your name]

---

**[The forwarding email they paste/send]:**

Subject: Intro: Vivekanand + lokul.club (pre-seed, India hyperlocal)

Hi [Investor],

Connecting you with Vivekanand Choudhari, founder of lokul.club — a hyperlocal community platform for Indian neighborhoods. He's building the verified neighborhood OS that turns the chaos of building WhatsApp groups into structured, transactable local commerce.

Early pilot traction, raising $750K pre-seed. Given your interest in [community/marketplace/India consumer], I thought it was worth a conversation.

Vivekanand, over to you.

[Connector name]

---

**Why this works:**
- You're doing the work for the connector — they just copy-paste
- The forwarding email is < 75 words — easy to skim and forward
- "Over to you" puts the ball in your court cleanly

## Template 3: Post-Event / Post-Meeting Follow-Up

**When to use:** You met the investor briefly at a conference, demo day, or networking event. They said "send me something."

---

**Subject:** lokul.club — great meeting you at [Event name]

**Body:**

Hi [Name],

Good meeting you at [Event] yesterday. As promised — lokul.club in one paragraph:

We're building India's neighborhood OS — a geo-verified platform where residents transact with neighbors and local merchants. Think MyGate's trust layer plus Urban Company's service marketplace plus the kirana's loyal customer base, in one product. Raising $750K pre-seed; [traction line].

I'll keep this short — deck attached [only attach if they specifically asked] / [OR: happy to share the deck if you'd like a closer look].

Would a 30-minute call work this week?

[Your name]
[Phone]

---

**Why this works:**
- References the context immediately — they met 50 people that day, remind them who you are
- The one-paragraph summary is the verbal version of what you told them, now in writing
- Keeps the same "one ask" discipline

## Template 4: Follow-Up After No Response (10–12 Days Later)

**When to use:** You sent Template 1 or 3. No reply. One follow-up only.

---

**Subject:** Re: lokul — quick follow-up

**Body:**

Hi [Name],

Following up on my note from [date] — wanted to share one update in case it's useful context:

[One new, specific data point: e.g., "We activated 2 new lokuls last week, bringing total to X societies and Y households. First GMV crossed ₹Z."]

If the timing isn't right, totally understand — but if you have 20 minutes to hear the story, I think it's worth it.

[Your name]

---

**Why this works:**
- One follow-up only — more than one becomes pestering
- New information justifies the follow-up (not "just checking in")
- Short — respects their time
- Gives them an easy out ("if timing isn't right") which paradoxically increases reply rate

## Template 5: The "Raise is Closing" Email

**When to use:** You have partial commitments, round is 50–70% closed, creating urgency for remaining investors.

---

**Subject:** lokul pre-seed — round closing, last spots

**Body:**

Hi [Name],

Quick update on lokul.club: our pre-seed round is ₹X committed of ₹Y target, with 2–3 spots remaining. Closing in 3 weeks.

If you've been thinking about it, now is the time to have the conversation. Happy to jump on a call this week.

[Traction update: one new number since last contact]

[Your name]

---

**Why this works:**
- Creates legitimate urgency (only use when true)
- Extremely short — urgency emails should be the shortest
- One new traction number keeps it relevant, not just pressure

## Subject Line Options — Test These

The subject line is 80% of whether the email gets opened.

**Insight-led (best for cold):**
- `lokul — India's neighborhood economy has no OS yet`
- `90M urban households, no trusted local commerce layer`
- `MyGate proved the market. lokul monetizes it.`

**Metric-led (best when you have numbers):**
- `lokul — ₹[X] GMV, [Y] societies, raising $750K pre-seed`
- `[X] households, [Y]% retention, raising pre-seed`

**Direct / simple (often outperforms clever):**
- `lokul.club — pre-seed, $750K, India hyperlocal`
- `Intro: lokul.club (pre-seed)`

**Avoid:**
- "Exciting opportunity" — sounds like spam
- "Quick question" — overused
- Long subject lines (>60 characters get cut on mobile)
- All caps anywhere

## The Research You Must Do Before Sending Any Email

For each investor, spend 10 minutes finding:

| What to find | Where to look | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| One portfolio company that relates | Their website / Crunchbase | "Your investment in X suggests..." |
| Their stated thesis | Twitter / blog / podcast | "I know you believe Y — lokul is that thesis applied to..." |
| A recent investment or tweet | Twitter / LinkedIn | Makes the email feel current, not templated |
| Whether they lead or follow | Crunchbase round data | Tailor your ask — "would you consider leading" vs. "are you open to participating" |
| Check size range | Publicly available / ask mutual connections | Don't ask a ₹25L angel for $750K |

**Do not send a batch email.** Investors talk to each other. A templated email that gets forwarded among a group of investors is worse than no email.

## The Investor List Strategy

Before writing a single email, build the list in tiers:

**Tier 1 — Warm connections (email last)**
Investors you have a mutual connection to. Approach these through the warm intro path. Save them for when your pitch is sharpest.

**Tier 2 — Thesis match, no connection (email second)**
Investors whose portfolio suggests they understand community, hyperlocal, or India consumer. These are worth a well-crafted cold email.

**Tier 3 — Long shots / learning conversations (email first)**
Investors you're less certain about. Email these first to practice and refine your pitch before approaching Tier 1.

**The order matters:** Build conviction and refine your pitch with Tier 3, close Tier 1 last when you have momentum.

## The One-Paragraph Version — Memorize This

Every email, every intro, every event conversation needs this. Write it. Refine it. Memorize it.

> "lokul.club is a geo-verified neighborhood platform for India — we turn the chaos of building WhatsApp groups into structured, trusted, local commerce. A resident finds their neighbor's home cook, books a local tutor, and orders from their kirana — all from people they already live near. We're building what MyGate proved was possible but never monetized. Raising $750K pre-seed to activate 500 neighborhoods across 3 cities."

**67 words. Practice until you can say it without thinking.**

## After You Send — What to Track

Keep a simple log for every email sent:

| Investor | Fund | Sent date | Connection type | Response | Next step | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | Cold / Warm / Event | | | What worked, what didn't |

After 20 emails, look at response rates by subject line and opening line. The version that gets replies is the version to keep.
